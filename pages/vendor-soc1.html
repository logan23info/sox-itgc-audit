Vendor & SOC 1 review guide

When a vendor processes financially significant data, their controls become your controls. This guide covers how to evaluate SOC 1 reports, identify CUECs, test your implementation, and document reliance — all per PCAOB AS 2601.

AS 2601 aligned SOC 1 Type I & II CUEC testing
PCAOB AS 2601: When a service organization processes transactions or maintains records that are part of the company's information systems, the auditor must evaluate the controls at that service organization. A SOC 1 Type II report is the primary mechanism for obtaining this evidence.

SOC 1 Type I vs Type II

Type I: Controls are suitably designed as of a specific date. No operating effectiveness testing. Limited SOX reliance value.

Type II: Controls operated effectively over a period (minimum 6 months). This is what SOX auditors need. Confirm the period covers your fiscal year.

Carve-out vs. inclusive method

Carve-out (common): Vendor's controls excluded from your scope. You test CUECs on your side. Vendor's SOC 1 is supplementary evidence.

Inclusive (rare): Vendor's controls included in your integrated audit scope. Requires coordination with the vendor's auditor.

What CUECs are

Complementary User Entity Controls — controls the SOC 1 report assumes your organization is operating. If you don't test your CUECs, you cannot rely on the SOC 1 report. This is the most commonly missed step.

Step-by-step

How to review a SOC 1 report

1

Obtain the current SOC 1 Type II report

Request from the vendor. Confirm it is a Type II (not Type I). Verify the report period covers your entire fiscal year — if there's a gap (e.g. report covers Jan–Sep but your FY ends Dec), request a bridge letter for the gap period or perform additional procedures.

2

Confirm the opinion is unqualified

Check Section 4 (the auditor's opinion). A qualified opinion means the service auditor found exceptions — these must be evaluated for impact on your controls. A qualified opinion is not automatically a deal-breaker but requires escalation and documentation.

3

Map vendor controls to your ITGC domains

In Section 5 (control description) and Section 6 (test results), identify which controls relate to Logical Access, Change Management, Computer Operations, and Program Development for the systems your company uses. Not all vendor controls are relevant to your scope.

4

Review test results for exceptions

For each relevant control, check whether the service auditor noted any exceptions. An exception means the control did not operate effectively 100% of the time. Evaluate the nature, frequency, and financial impact of each exception before concluding on reliance.

5

Identify and list all CUECs

CUECs are listed in Section 3 of the report. Extract every CUEC that relates to your use of the vendor's system. These are controls the report assumes you are operating. Each CUEC becomes a testing item in your own audit program.

6

Test each CUEC

For each CUEC: document the control you have in place, obtain evidence it operated effectively, and note it in your workpaper. Common CUECs include: access reviews for your user accounts in the vendor system, review of exception reports produced by the vendor, and monitoring of data feeds between systems.

7

Document the reliance conclusion

Write a formal conclusion: the SOC 1 Type II report for [Vendor] for the period [X] to [Y] was reviewed. The opinion was unqualified. [N] exceptions were noted — impact assessed as [immaterial / requires additional testing]. CUECs identified and tested — see workpaper ref [X]. Reliance on vendor controls is [placed / not placed] for the following reasons: [...]

No SOC 1 available? If a vendor processes financially material data but has no SOC 1, you have three options: (1) perform direct testing at the vendor site, (2) request the vendor obtain a SOC 1 as a contractual requirement, or (3) design compensating controls on your side that reduce reliance on the vendor's unattested controls. All three options require documentation and discussion with your external auditor.
