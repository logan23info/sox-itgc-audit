Control remediation

ITGC remediation playbook

When a control fails, classification is just the beginning. This playbook walks you through exactly what to do next — from immediate response through re-testing and closure — for every severity level.

PCAOB AS 2201.62–.70 Timeline guidance Communication templates Re-testing criteria
Severity levels

Understand your classification before you remediate

The remediation approach — and urgency — differs fundamentally by classification. Get this right first.

Control deficiency (CD)

A gap in design or operation that doesn't meet the MW or SD threshold. Low likelihood of a material misstatement even if the control failed.

  • No required communication to audit committee
  • Document in internal deficiency log
  • Remediate within next audit cycle
  • No public disclosure required
Low urgency AS 2201.62

Significant deficiency (SD)

Less severe than MW but important enough for the audit committee to be aware of. More than remote possibility of misstatement but below material threshold.

  • Must communicate in writing to audit committee
  • Communication required before fiscal year-end
  • Include in management's 404(a) discussion
  • Track remediation status formally
High urgency AS 2201.68

Material weakness (MW)

Reasonable possibility of material misstatement not prevented or detected on a timely basis. The most serious classification — requires public disclosure.

  • Immediate audit committee escalation
  • Public disclosure in annual 10-K (Item 9A)
  • External auditor must also disclose
  • CEO/CFO §302 certification implications
Critical urgency AS 2201.69–.70
Step-by-step playbooks

Remediation by classification

A control deficiency does not require communication to the audit committee or public disclosure. Handle internally with discipline and structure.

Immediate response (Day 1–3)

Days 1–3
1
Document the deficiency formally

Enter the deficiency in the deficiency log with: control ID, description of the failure, date identified, root cause (preliminary), and severity classification. Assign a unique reference number (e.g., DEF-2026-001).

Same day as identification
2
Identify the control owner

Confirm who owns the control and notify them of the deficiency. The control owner is responsible for remediation — Internal Audit facilitates and tracks but does not remediate (independence).

3
Perform root cause analysis

Determine why the control failed — was it a design gap, a resource gap, a training gap, or a process breakdown? The root cause drives the remediation approach. Fix symptoms without addressing root cause and the deficiency recurs.

Remediation planning (Week 1–2)

Week 1–2
1
Define the remediation action

Based on root cause: redesign the control, update the procedure, provide training, implement a new tool, or restructure responsibilities. Document the specific action, owner, and target completion date.

2
Consider interim compensating controls

If the control cannot be fixed immediately, implement a compensating control to reduce risk in the interim. Document the compensating control and test its operating effectiveness. Compensating controls do not close the deficiency — they manage risk while remediation proceeds.

3
Set re-testing milestone

Agree on when re-testing will occur. For a CD, re-testing typically occurs in the next quarterly or annual testing cycle. Build re-testing into the audit plan.

Evidence and closure

Post-remediation
1
Obtain remediation evidence

Document that the remediation action was completed — updated policy, training completion record, system configuration change, new process documentation. Evidence must show the root cause was addressed, not just the symptom.

2
Re-test the control

Perform testing to confirm the control now operates as designed. For a control deficiency, a limited re-test (1–2 cycles) may be sufficient. Document the re-test separately from the original workpaper.

3
Close in deficiency log

Update the deficiency log with remediation date, re-test results, and closure conclusion. Include a note on what was done and the re-test outcome. Internal Audit signs off on closure.

A significant deficiency must be communicated in writing to the audit committee before the end of the fiscal year. This is a required step — not optional — under PCAOB AS 2201.68 and IIA Standard 2060.

Immediate response (Day 1)

Day 1
1
Escalate to CAE and CFO immediately

A significant deficiency must reach the Chief Audit Executive (CAE) and CFO on the day it is identified. Do not wait for the next scheduled reporting meeting. Verbal notification first, followed by a formal written summary within 24 hours.

Day 1
2
Notify external auditors

External auditors must be made aware of the significant deficiency promptly — they cannot issue their opinion without understanding all identified deficiencies. Coordinate the timing of communication with management and the external auditor.

3
Implement interim compensating controls immediately

For an SD, compensating controls are not optional — implement them on Day 1. These reduce the risk of a material misstatement while full remediation proceeds and demonstrate management's commitment to the audit committee.

Audit committee communication (before year-end)

Before FY-end
1
Prepare written communication for audit committee

The communication must include: description of the significant deficiency, the potential effect on financial reporting, management's remediation plan, and timeline. This is a formal document — not an email summary.

2
Present at audit committee meeting

CAE or CFO presents the deficiency at the next audit committee meeting (or a special meeting if the next one is after year-end). Audit committee must have opportunity to ask questions and receive management's remediation plan.

3
Document that communication occurred

Retain the written communication, the meeting minutes confirming the deficiency was discussed, and any audit committee questions and management responses. This documentation is evidence for both management and the external auditor.

Remediation and re-testing

60–120 days
1
Develop a formal remediation plan with milestones

A significant deficiency requires a project-managed remediation plan — not just a task list. Define milestones, owners, dependencies, and a target date for re-testing. Report progress to the CAE and CFO weekly.

2
Re-test after minimum operation period

After remediation is implemented, the control must demonstrate operating effectiveness for a sufficient period before re-testing. For monthly controls, 2–3 months of operation. For daily controls, 30 days. Do not re-test immediately after implementation.

Minimum 60–90 days of operation before re-test
3
Report remediation status at next audit committee meeting

Update the audit committee on remediation progress at the next scheduled meeting. If the SD is remediated before year-end, the external auditor may be able to issue a clean opinion. If it persists to year-end, it must appear in the 404(a) assessment.

A material weakness requires public disclosure in the annual 10-K (Item 9A). CEO and CFO cannot certify that ICFR is effective under §302. External auditors issue an adverse opinion on ICFR. Treat this as a crisis-level response.

Crisis response (Day 1)

Day 1 — crisis
1
Immediate escalation to CEO, CFO, General Counsel, and Board

A material weakness is a board-level event. Notify the CEO, CFO, General Counsel, and Audit Committee Chair on Day 1 — the same day identification is confirmed. Do not delay for additional analysis. Get the right people in the room immediately.

Same day
2
Notify external auditors and engage legal counsel

External auditors must be informed immediately — they are required to evaluate the MW as part of their integrated audit and issue an adverse ICFR opinion. Legal counsel should be engaged to advise on disclosure obligations and timing.

3
Assess the financial statement impact

Determine whether the material weakness resulted in any actual financial misstatements requiring restatement. This analysis must be completed before the 10-K is filed. The SEC may require a Form 8-K if the MW affects previously filed financial statements.

4
Implement emergency compensating controls

Immediately implement the strongest possible compensating controls to reduce the risk of a material misstatement for the remainder of the year. Document these controls and begin testing their effectiveness. They will be disclosed alongside the MW.

Disclosure and communication (before 10-K filing)

Before 10-K filing
1
Draft 10-K Item 9A disclosure language

The 10-K must disclose: (1) that a material weakness exists, (2) the nature and description of the MW, (3) the potential effect on ICFR, and (4) management's remediation plan. Work with legal counsel and external auditors to finalize language. SEC staff review these disclosures carefully.

2
Prepare CEO/CFO §302 certifications

The CEO and CFO cannot certify that ICFR is effective when a MW exists. The §302 certification language must be modified to disclose the MW. Legal counsel must review the final certification language.

3
Coordinate external auditor adverse opinion

External auditors will issue an adverse opinion on ICFR — this is a required outcome. Coordinate timing, language alignment, and the management representation letter. The external auditor's adverse opinion language must be consistent with management's disclosure.

Sustained remediation (3–12 months)

3–12 months
1
Build a board-level remediation program

A material weakness remediation is a C-suite initiative, not an IT project. Assign an executive sponsor (typically CFO or CIO). Establish a remediation steering committee with weekly status reporting. Resource it adequately — MW remediation consistently requires more time and money than initially planned.

2
Redesign the control environment — not just the failing control

Material weaknesses often signal broader control environment issues. Address the root cause at the system and process level, not just the specific control. Evaluate whether similar gaps exist in related controls. A narrow fix that leaves underlying vulnerabilities is likely to produce another MW.

3
Demonstrate sustained operation before claiming remediation

The remediated control must operate effectively for a sufficient period — typically a full quarter or two quarterly cycles minimum — before management can assert the MW is remediated. External auditors require evidence of sustained operation, not just implementation.

Minimum 90–180 days of operation before re-test
4
Update 10-K/10-Q disclosure as remediation progresses

If the MW is remediated before the next annual report, disclose the remediation in the next 10-Q or 10-K. If it persists into the next year, it must be re-disclosed. The company's credibility depends on transparent, accurate disclosure of remediation status.

Re-testing a remediated control too early is one of the most common remediation mistakes. A control implemented yesterday has not demonstrated sustained operating effectiveness — auditors require evidence of operation over a meaningful period.

What makes a re-test valid

  • Control has been implemented (not just designed)
  • Minimum operation period has elapsed
  • Root cause has been addressed — not just symptoms
  • Re-test is performed by someone independent of remediation
  • Full standard sample size selected (no shortcuts)
  • Re-test workpaper is separate from original workpaper
  • Zero exceptions in re-test (one exception extends timeline)

What extends the remediation timeline

  • Any exception in the re-test — restart the clock
  • Control owner changes during the remediation period
  • Related control failure identified during re-testing
  • Process change that affects control design mid-remediation
  • External auditor identifies additional scope of the deficiency
  • Re-test performed too early (before min. operation period)
Root cause analysis

Common root causes and remediation approaches

The right remediation action depends entirely on the root cause. Using the wrong fix is the most common reason deficiencies recur.

Lack of awarenessControl owner didn't know they owned it or what evidence was requiredUpdate the RCMControl owner training + accountability framework + evidence checklist
